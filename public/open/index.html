How calls are scored

Each call is a probability of YES. Once a question resolves, its Brier loss is (probability − outcome)², using probabilities from 0 to 1 and outcomes of 0 or 1. Lower is better: 80% on a YES result loses 0.04; 80% on a NO result loses 0.64.

Your score is averaged over the entire open window. Each revision counts only from its server acceptance time onward. Before your first call, we use a neutral 50%. You must participate for at least half the window to qualify for ranking. Each eligible resolved question has equal weight. Inconclusive questions are excluded.

Signed submissions, server timestamps, question commitments and all revisions are public. The pilot service timestamps and stores records; this is not an onchain timestamp guarantee. Lightweight browser identities and rate limits discourage casual spam but cannot prove one human per identity.

Research points recognize mathematical work. Forecast scores recognize accuracy. Forecasts earn no PNP points, prizes or redeemable rewards. There are no wagers, deposits or tradable positions.

Incomparable encodings for hash-chain signatures

Encode a b-bit digest as one position on each of k hash chains of length w − 1. Signing reveals the chosen elements. Verification hashes each one forward to its chain endpoint.

E : {0, 1}b → {0, …, w − 1}k

For any distinct digests x and y, require a coordinate where E(x) is smaller than E(y) and another where it is larger. The resulting codewords form an antichain. Moving forward on the chains alone cannot turn one codeword into another.

In this model, signing takes ∑i E(x)i chain steps and verification takes ∑i (w − 1 − E(x)i). The signature reveals k elements. Count the work of computing E separately. Key generation, authentication paths, message hashing and aggregation overhead fall outside these counts.

The known baseline and the open question

With a 256-bit digest and 16 positions per chain, WOTS+ uses 64 message digits and three checksum digits. At 32 bytes per element, those 67 elements occupy 2,144 bytes before authentication paths. RFC 8391, §3.1

For a deterministic, injective encoding with equally long chains, the largest antichain is a middle rank of the product order. Its size is the central coefficient of (1 + z + … + z15)k.

What an aggregation proof establishes

For N signers, the basic relation requires VerifyS(pki, mi, σi) for every signer i. A consensus application must also bind the signer set, message or epoch, voting weights and quorum rule.

Report proof size, signature size, proving work, verification work, encoding work and security loss separately. A formal security claim needs an adversary model, hash assumptions, key-use rules and message and domain binding. Incomparability proves one combinatorial property.

Research directions and evidence
  • Incomparable encodings. Construct an efficient encoder, or prove a tighter size–time trade-off under explicit constraints.
  • Proof-friendly verification. Prove a cheaper representation of a batch of signature checks while preserving the verification relation.
  • Security reductions. Bound the security loss under a specified classical or quantum adversary model.
  • Limits and counterexamples. Prove a lower bound or exhibit a checked counterexample to a proposed mathematical construction.

A submission should identify the exact theorem, assumptions, cost model, relevant prior work and contribution. The verifier checks the submitted statement in Lean and Mathlib. The PQC contract reviews usefulness and assigns research credit. A formalization of known work should credit its original authors. Operation counts alone establish no measured speedup in a blockchain client.

Prior art and implementations
  • RFC 8391 · XMSS and WOTS+ — chain parameters and the one-time signature baseline.
  • Hash-Based Multi-Signatures for Post-Quantum Ethereum — a framework for validator signatures and aggregation.
  • At the Top of the Hypercube — encoding constructions and size–time bounds.
  • Technical Note: LeanSig for Post-Quantum Ethereum — parameters and encodings for the Ethereum research setting.
  • leanSig and leanVM — signature and proof-system implementation references. Pin the revision and parameters used for any comparison.

Easy to check.
Easy to find?

If a solution can be checked efficiently, can it also be found efficiently? That is the question behind P versus NP. We do not know the answer.

This is a place to work toward either answer, one useful result at a time. A small theorem is not a solution to the whole problem. A failed approach can still teach us something.

How GenLayer reviews research

A proof can be correct and still repeat a known result. Lean checks what a theorem proves. Each track’s Intelligent Contract on GenLayer Studio assesses what the contribution adds to that track’s research.

Current proof format, challenges & pilot limits

The reviewed problem policy specifies the pinned Lean environment and accepted proof format. Research notes are explicitly nonformal. Each proof establishes its exact submitted statement.

The application challenge window is five minutes. Up to two evidence-backed application challenges can pause a decision for independent review before settlement. The Studio appeal guide explains the transaction deadline. Settled points do not yet have a correction mechanism.

Retrospective credit currently covers verified direct formal proof use. Citation-only influence, general semantic graph relationships and cross-ledger rewards are not automated. A positive live downstream point award and the full application challenge flow remain to be demonstrated.

The PR author must post the exact wallet-signed claim in an unedited comment. The verifier matches GitHub account IDs and verifies the wallet signature. GitHub usernames are separately verified and recorded on chain. Account age and one active claim per GitHub author provide basic spam controls. They do not establish independent humans or complete Sybil resistance. Duplicate checks are bounded, not a comprehensive literature review. The pilot trusts reviewed verifier and executor roles and repository administration.

This is a static site backed by native ledger reads and GitHub Actions. Published snapshots are dated, limited to the newest twelve records, and cannot authorize merges or points. Live reads are collected over an interval, not as an atomic snapshot.
